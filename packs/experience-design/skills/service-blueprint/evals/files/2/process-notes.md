# Delivery and assembly — how it works today (written by the Operations Manager, Oakhollow Home)

Oakhollow Home sells sofas, beds and wardrobes from 11 showrooms. Customers can add "Delivery + Assembly" at checkout. From order to assembled furniture takes about 5 days end to end.

1. **Order placed.** Customer buys in a showroom (sales associate keys it into the till system) or online (web shop). Customer gets an order confirmation email with "We'll be in touch to book your delivery".
2. **Stock allocated.** Overnight batch in the warehouse management system (WMS) allocates stock. If an item is out of stock it goes on back-order; nobody is notified automatically — the warehouse team checks a back-order report on Mondays.
3. **Slot booking call.** Our warehouse team phones the customer to agree a delivery day. They use the courier's slot calendar, which they log into separately.
4. **Picking and loading.** Warehouse picks the order the day before delivery and loads the courier's van.
5. **Delivery day.** The courier (Brightlane Logistics, a subcontractor) texts the customer a 4-hour window the evening before. Two-person crew delivers and assembles.
6. **Damaged on arrival.** If the item is damaged, the crew takes photos and leaves it, or takes it away. Someone then arranges a replacement — I'm honestly not sure who owns this; it moves between Customer Service and the warehouse.
7. **Sign-off.** Customer signs on the crew's handheld. Proof of delivery syncs to the courier's system, not ours. Customer Service sees it only if they ask Brightlane.
