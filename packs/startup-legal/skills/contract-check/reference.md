# The clauses that hurt a small company

For each: what to look for, why it hurts a startup, and wording to ask for. The wording is a
starting position for a redline or an email, not a finished clause; adapt the numbers to the
deal. "Founder" below means the founder's company, whichever side of the deal it is on.

## Liability

- **Look for:** a cap on one side only; a cap tied to a tiny figure (one month's fees);
  exclusions ("indirect", "consequential", "loss of profit") that apply to one party; carve-outs
  from the cap so wide they swallow it (all breaches of confidentiality, all data incidents).
- **Why it hurts:** a small supplier with uncapped liability can lose the company on one claim.
- **Ask for:** *"Each party's total liability arising out of or in connection with this
  Agreement is limited to the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the event giving
  rise to the claim. Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss, or loss of
  profit."* Keep death and personal injury from negligence, and fraud, unlimited: the law
  won't let either party exclude those in most countries, and asking to only weakens the ask.

## Indemnities

- **Look for:** "indemnify, defend and hold harmless" running from the founder only; an
  indemnity for "any claim arising from use of the Services" (which includes the customer's own
  misuse); indemnities outside the liability cap.
- **Ask for:** mutual indemnities limited to the indemnifier's own breach, negligence or IP
  infringement; the indemnified party must notify promptly and let the indemnifier run the
  defence; indemnities sit inside the cap, or under a separate cap of 2–3× fees.

## Intellectual property

- **Look for:** "all work product, inventions and improvements" assigned to the customer,
  without excluding the founder's pre-existing technology; a licence back that ends with the
  contract; feedback clauses that hand over the product roadmap.
- **Why it hurts:** a consulting gig that assigns the founder's core code to one customer kills
  the product and any later fundraise (investors check this in diligence).
- **Ask for:** *"Each party keeps its Background IP. The Customer owns the Deliverables
  created specifically for it under this Agreement, excluding the Supplier's Background IP and
  general know-how, which the Supplier licenses to the Customer on a non-exclusive, perpetual
  basis to use the Deliverables."*

## Payment

- **Look for:** payment terms over 45 days; payment "on acceptance" with no deemed-acceptance
  date; the customer's right to set off or withhold disputed amounts in full; no late-payment
  interest; fees fixed for a multi-year term with no increase.
- **Ask for:** 30 days from invoice; deemed acceptance 10 business days after delivery unless
  a written rejection with reasons; only the disputed portion can be withheld; interest on late
  payment; an annual increase of CPI or a stated percentage.

## Term, renewal and termination

- **Look for:** automatic renewal with a long notice window (60–90 days before the end);
  termination for convenience on one side only; no right to terminate for non-payment;
  minimum commitments that survive termination; payment of the whole term on early exit.
- **Ask for:** renewal notice of 30 days; termination for convenience for both parties, or for
  neither; termination on 30 days' notice for an unremedied material breach, including
  non-payment. Work out the renewal notice date and put it in the calendar.

## Exclusivity, non-compete and non-solicit

- **Look for:** "the Supplier shall not provide similar services to any competitor of the
  Customer" (which may be the founder's whole market); non-solicitation of employees that runs
  one way or lasts years after the contract.
- **Ask for:** no exclusivity, or exclusivity limited to a named list of competitors, a field
  and a term no longer than the contract, paid for. Mutual non-solicit for 12 months, not
  catching general job advertisements.

## Confidentiality

- **Look for:** obligations on the founder only; no exceptions for information already public,
  already known or independently developed; no time limit; a definition broad enough to cover
  the founder's own product.
- **Ask for:** mutual obligations; the standard exceptions; a term of 3–5 years after the
  agreement ends (trade secrets for as long as they remain secret).

## Data protection

- **Look for:** personal data passing between the parties with no data processing terms;
  unlimited audit rights; breach notification in 24 hours; liability for data incidents
  outside the cap.
- **Ask for:** a data processing agreement or annex when either party processes personal data
  for the other; breach notice "without undue delay, and in any case within 72 hours"; audits
  once a year on 30 days' notice, at the requester's cost, satisfied first by existing
  certifications.

## Governing law, disputes, assignment

- **Look for:** the other side's home courts on another continent; mandatory arbitration with
  a large-institution fee schedule; a ban on assignment that blocks a sale of the founder's
  company.
- **Ask for:** the founder's home law, or a neutral one; courts rather than arbitration for
  small contracts; either party may assign to a buyer of all or substantially all of its
  business without consent.

## Warranties and service levels

- **Look for:** "fit for purpose" or "error-free" warranties; service credits that are not the
  sole remedy; uptime above what the founder's own infrastructure provider promises.
- **Ask for:** services provided "with reasonable skill and care, materially in accordance with
  the Documentation"; service credits as the sole and exclusive remedy for missed service
  levels; uptime at or below the hosting provider's own commitment.

## When a lawyer earns the fee

Name these only when they appear in the document:
- an IP assignment of anything beyond deliverables made for this customer;
- a personal guarantee, or a founder signing in a personal capacity;
- uncapped liability or an indemnity outside the cap that the other side refuses to move on;
- a non-compete or exclusivity that covers the founder's market;
- an agreement under a law the founder has never dealt with, for a meaningful amount.
