# Chairside — clinic manager interviews, no-shows

Interviewer: Tomas Reyes (PM). Six 30-minute calls, 13–24 Oct 2026. Notes are mine, not transcripts; quotes are as close as I could get them.

Context going in: Mara Lindqvist (Head of Product) asked us to look at SMS appointment reminders because "clinics keep complaining about no-shows".

---

## A — Oakfield Family Dental (acct CL-1043), 3 chairs
Manager: Helen Brandt

- Biggest headache is the afternoon slots. "Half the time they just forgot. We ring the day before if we have time, which we usually don't."
- Would like an automatic reminder. Asked if it could be a text, "nobody reads email".
- Reschedule calls are "fine, we get a few".
- Q: how many no-shows? "Feels like a lot. Maybe one or two a day? I've never counted."

## B — Riverside Smiles (acct CL-1107), 5 chairs
Manager: Owen Pak

- No-shows mostly from patients who knew they couldn't make it but "didn't want to ring". "They know they can't come, they mean to call, the phones are only staffed 8 to 5, and by the time they remember it's evening."
- Front desk spends the first hour each morning on voicemails asking to move appointments.
- Wants patients to be able to move their own appointment. "Not cancel — move. If they can only cancel, I've lost the slot and the patient."
- Reminders: "We already send the default email reminder from Chairside. Doesn't change much."

## C — Harbourside Dental Studio (acct CL-1122), 4 chairs
Manager: Priyanka Shah

- Already sends SMS reminders through a separate tool (pays for it separately, wants to drop it).
- Still gets no-shows. "The text goes out at 7pm the night before. They reply 'can I move this?' to the text. Nobody sees that reply, it's a no-reply number. Then they don't come."
- "Reminders aren't the problem. The problem is there's nothing they can do with the reminder."
- Wants: the reminder to let the patient pick a new time.

## D — Northgate Dental Care (acct CL-1158), 2 chairs
Manager: Femi Adeyemi

- Small practice, older patient base. "A lot of my patients don't text."
- Forgetting is the main reason. Calls every patient the day before; takes about 40 minutes a day.
- Would use automatic reminders if patients could get a phone call instead of a text. Not sure SMS helps his patients.
- Rescheduling: "They call, it's fine."

## E — Lakeview Orthodontics (acct CL-1201), 6 chairs
Manager: Grace Muller

- Patients are mostly teenagers; parents book. Parents "can't call during work hours".
- No-shows cluster on school-holiday weeks, when plans change. Parents want to move the appointment and "the only way is to phone us".
- Wants online rescheduling, limited to the same clinician. Orthodontic appointments must stay with the same clinician.
- Asked whether a reminder would help: "A bit. But they don't forget, they just can't get through to us."

## F — Westbrook Dental (acct CL-1219), 4 chairs
Manager: Ana Kowalczyk

- "When a patient no-shows I can almost always see a missed call or a voicemail from the day before." Has started checking.
- Front desk is one person; can't answer while checking someone in.
- Wants patients to move appointments themselves, but worried about double-booking and about patients moving into slots reserved for emergencies.
- Indifferent on reminders. "Fine, but that's not what's happening."

---

## My quick read (not analysed yet)
- I went in expecting "forgetting" and I'm not sure that's the main thing.
- Two clinics (A, D) say forgetting. Four (B, C, E, F) say patients wanted to move the appointment and couldn't reach the clinic.
- D's patients may not use SMS at all.
- Nobody gave me a real no-show number. None of them track it.
