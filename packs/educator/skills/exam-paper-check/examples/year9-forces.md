# Worked example: a Year 9 forces test

## Input

> *"Can you check my Year 9 forces and energy test before I send it to the printers?
> [inputs/year9-science.md](inputs/year9-science.md), mark scheme
> [inputs/year9-science-scheme.md](inputs/year9-science-scheme.md), and our planning grid is
> AO1 40 %, AO2 40 %, AO3 20 %."*

The spec was written from the planning grid ([inputs/spec.json](inputs/spec.json)).

## Reply

**Four things to fix before it goes to print:**

1. **The cover says 50 marks, but the paper is worth 41.** Section A adds up to 29 and Section
   B to 12 (a student answers Question 6 *or* 7). Either change the cover to 41, or add 9 marks.
2. **Questions 6 and 7 are an either/or but aren't worth the same:** 12 and 10. Make both 12
   (the mark scheme already gives 7 twelve marks).
3. **Question 3 goes from (a) to (c).** Reletter (c) and (d) as (b) and (c), and the scheme to match.
4. **The mark scheme has nothing for 2(b)** (reducing measurement error, 1 mark). Add: *"repeat
   and take a mean / use a set square or eye level with the top of the ball (1)"*.

**Balance.** Against your grid, AO1 has 27 % (target 40), AO2 71 % (40) and AO3 2 % (20).
Section B's 12 marks all sit in AO2. Crediting 6 of Question 6's 12 marks to an AO3 judgement
strand, and making 2(b) a 3-mark planning question, would give AO3 9 of 43 marks (21 %).

**Time.** 50 minutes for 41 marks is 1.2 minutes a mark, generous for Year 9; at 50 marks it
would be 1.0.

**Wording**
- 1(b): give the formula sheet reference, or the formula, if students aren't expected to recall it.
- 4: "Include a risk assessment" carries no marks of its own in the scheme; say how it is credited.
- 5(b): "Sketch" for 2 marks: say what earns each mark (axes labelled, straight line).
