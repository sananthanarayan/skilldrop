# Fernhill Community College
## Year 11 Chemistry Mock - Paper 1: Quantitative Chemistry and Rates

Time allowed: 50 minutes
Total marks: 44

**Instructions**
- Answer all questions in the spaces provided.
- Calculators must not be used in this paper.
- Relative atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, Ca = 40

---

### Question 1
1(a) State the law of conservation of mass. [1]
1(b) Magnesium burns in oxygen: 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO. Calculate the relative formula mass (Mr) of magnesium oxide. [2]
1(c) 4.8 g of magnesium is burned completely in oxygen. Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed. [3]
1(d) When a student heated calcium carbonate in an open crucible, the mass of the crucible and contents went down. Explain why. [2]

Total for Question 1: 8 marks

### Question 2
2(a) Give the meaning of the term "concentration" and its units. [2]
2(b) 2.0 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in water to make 250 cm3 of solution. Calculate the concentration in g/dm3. [3]
2(c) Describe how you would make up exactly 250 cm3 of this solution in the laboratory. [4]

Total for Question 2: 8 marks

### Question 3
A student added marble chips to dilute hydrochloric acid and collected the gas.

| Time (s) | 0 | 37 | 60 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of CO2 (cm3) | 0.0 | 53.9 | 71.0 | 74.0 |

3(a) Give three ways the rate of this reaction could be measured. [3]
3(b) Explain, in terms of particles, why increasing the concentration of the acid increases the rate of reaction. [3]
3(c) Calculate the mean rate of reaction between 0 and 37 s, and the amount in moles of carbon dioxide collected in that time. Give both answers to 3 significant figures. (1 mol of any gas occupies 24.0 dm3 at room temperature and pressure.) [4]
3(d) Suggest why the volume of gas hardly changes after 60 s. [2]

Total for Question 3: 12 marks

### Question 5
5(a) Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction of calcium carbonate with hydrochloric acid. [2]
5(b) Calculate the Mr of calcium carbonate and the maximum mass of carbon dioxide produced from 10 g of calcium carbonate. [4]
5(c) A student collected 3.3 g of carbon dioxide. Calculate the percentage yield. [3]
5(d) State how adding a catalyst affects the rate of this reaction. [6]

Total for Question 5: 15 marks
